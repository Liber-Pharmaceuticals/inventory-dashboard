Weekly demand (v1.1 rolling, v4.1 integrity): for Sigma + Symbion = (units sold in last 3 full months + current partial month) ÷ (3 × 4.33 + elapsed weeks of current month). Window auto-rolls forward each weekly refresh. API uses its own avg weekly forecast. v4.1: months in which the wholesaler channel held less than ~1 week of stock are excluded from numerator and denominator alike, because a stockout suppresses recorded sales while calendar time keeps accruing — which would otherwise make a starved SKU look better covered than it is. Where exclusions leave fewer than three clean months the lookback extends back up to nine months and the figure is treated as indicative. Channel Wks Cover = W/Sale SOH (Sigma + Symbion + API) ÷ network demand — cover sitting in the pharmaceutical wholesale channel. 3PL Wks Cover = 3PL available (IVE) ÷ network demand — cover of stock in Liber's possession; this drives Status, KPI tiles and gap-risk (v4.0). Total Available = W/Sale SOH + 3PL available. Incoming PO = sum of confirmed/in-transit purchase order units (date = earliest Avail from 3PL). Backorders = wholesaler orders awaiting fulfilment (from the weekly Cin7 export) — shown negative because they are committed against the next landing. Net Cover w/PO = (3PL Avail + Incoming PO − Backorders) ÷ demand — the forward cover of stock in our possession once the PO lands and backorders are cleared (excludes channel SOH). Timeline = 26-week horizon; markers show projected 3PL stock-out date and PO landing date, assessed net of backorders. v4.2 — Status and the Order Date Passed / Order Within 2 Weeks tiles key off SLACK, the days until each SKU's sea order-by date, computed by a weekly forward simulation (committed POs land into 3PL, backorders clear, pharmacies buy through the channel, and the channel reorders toward the 5.5-week holding it self-selects when well supplied). On a 13.1-week lead time a SKU can hold twenty weeks of cover and still be late to order — distance-from-empty is not distance-from-decision. The full order-timing detail — Floor Breach, Order By (Sea/Air), Slack, draw regimes and the next-PO gate — lives on the Replenishment tab; this table keeps the stock and cover picture, and the timeline still shows 3PL run-out.

How each date is built. The model steps forward one week at a time for every SKU. In each week: committed purchase orders land into 3PL; open backorders are cleared out of 3PL first because they are already owed; pharmacies buy through the wholesale channel at the network demand rate; the channel then reorders from 3PL to restore the 5.5-week holding it self-selects when well supplied (measured at Sigma 6.1 weeks and Symbion 5.3 weeks on unconstrained SKUs); anything 3PL cannot supply accrues as new backorder. Floor Breach is the first week the channel's forward cover falls below 4 weeks because 3PL could not supply it. Breaches BEFORE the last committed landing are excluded — those belong to orders already in the water, not to the next one — so the date shown is always the first breach a new order could still prevent. Order By (Sea) is that date less 13.1 weeks: liquids 6 weeks (the binding constraint), cartridge manufacture 9 business days, 7 days to vessel, 16 days sea, 7 days Australian clearance and 3PL receipt. Air replaces the sea leg and saves about ten days — useful for a late spike, not for a line that is already months late. Freight policy is sea-only. Slack drives Status: negative = the order date has passed.
What the model does not do. It holds the demand rate flat at the v4.1 measured figure — it carries no growth assumption, so a SKU trending upward will breach earlier than shown. It assumes wholesalers keep ordering at the measured rate and to the same target holding; they set their own policies. It assumes committed POs land on their ledger dates, so a freight slip moves every date behind it. And per-SKU floors scaled by demand volatility are implemented but deliberately switched off, so every figure here is attributable to the depletion logic alone rather than to a floor that moved underneath it — the same discipline used for recency weighting in v4.1.
